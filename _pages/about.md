I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Engineering at the [University of Massachusetts Amherst](https://www.umass.edu/engineering/electrical-and-computer-engineering), advised by [Prof. David Irwin](https://www.davidirwin.info) and [Prof. Michael Zink](https://www.mikezink.net) in the [Sustainable Computing Lab](https://www.sustainablecomputinglab.io/). I build distributed systems that make large-scale ML training and inference more efficient under real-world resource constraints, including power, network bandwidth, energy, and carbon footprint.

The central question driving my research is: as AI workloads outgrow the power and compute capacity of any single datacenter, how do we efficiently distribute training and inference across heterogeneous, power-constrained sites? My work addresses this at multiple levels of the stack, from dynamic scheduling of geo-distributed GPU clusters for LLM training, to hierarchical communication topologies that improve energy efficiency of geo-distributed training, to carbon-aware scheduling that minimizes the environmental cost of federated learning. I have published at ACM SoCC, ACM/IEEE SEC, ICPE (Best Paper Runner-Up), AMS, and IEEE eScience.

During my Ph.D., I was a Research Scientist Intern at [Pacific Northwest National Laboratory (PNNL)](https://www.pnnl.gov/) in the Advanced Computing, Mathematics, and Data Division. I worked with [Dr. Nathan Tallent](https://www.pnnl.gov/people/nathan-tallent) and [Dr. Luanzheng Guo](https://lguo.net/) on distributed ML training systems for power-constrained environments. Prior to UMass, I completed my B.S. in Electrical Engineering at NUST Pakistan, where I received the Rector's Gold Medal for the best thesis in my graduating class.

I am actively looking for full-time positions in ML infrastructure and distributed systems, available from September 2026.
